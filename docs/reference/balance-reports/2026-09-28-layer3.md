## Collection model

What a typical player owns, drawn with the engine's own code:
- **Starting deck:** `getInitialDeck`, which holds one of every signature card.
- **Drops:** one per win, from `drawCard` with the monster as it is at that fight, so the level
  gate and the early drop boost are the engine's.
- **Assumptions:**
  - 50% wins.
  - 9 XP a fight, measured in production at levels 1–3.
  - No shop buys, so this is a floor.
  - 200 players per monster.

That gives, by level: L1 4 fights, 22 cards; L3 13, 26; L5 43, 42; L7 117, 79; L10 495, 269;
L12 1,295, about 670.

Each cell is the typical inventory, then the unconstrained one. Both are the best 9 cards the
monster can hold, each at most 4 copies (`MAX_CARD_COPIES_IN_HAND`), summed by catalogue sHE.
The typical inventory is drawn from the collection; the unconstrained one from every card.

## Findings

1. **The Weeping Angel's body is by far the weakest.**
   - With the same hand it scores 7% at level 0 and 18–28% after, about 2.5–5 Hits below
     the reference.
   - Its strength is all in its cards. Blink is worth 3.3 Hits on the Angel at level 1, and
     Blast II and Enchanted Faceswap 2.0–2.5.
   - So random hands looked fine and informed hands ran away (83–89% late in the corrected
     curves). How good the Angel is depends on its hand more than any other monster's.
   - Scaling the Angel down as a whole would crush random-hand Angels. The balance lever is
     its handful of cards, or its body.
2. **The Dragon is weak in body and in cards.**
   - Its body is 1–2.5 Hits below the reference at every level.
   - Tsunami, its epic, is worth nothing on the Dragon too (−0.2–0.1). Mood Scales is
     0.1–0.6.
   - Take Wing (1.9–2.1) and Fire Breath (1.2–1.9) are fine but cannot carry that body.
   - Candidates: a body adjustment, or fixing Tsunami. Both are for the next pass.
3. **The Unicorn's body is the strongest, and its cards are the weakest.**
   - Its body is +0.3 to +1.4 Hits.
   - Unconquerable Horn is worth about 0 on the Unicorn, Dissonant Voice −0.3–0.3, Gloaming
     Rest about 0, and Horn of Proof 0.2–0.5. Only Sticketh (1.4–1.6) of its own cards pulls
     weight.
   - Its mid-level weakness (25–31%) is an inventory problem, so fix the cards, not the body.
4. **The Jinn's body is weak early and average from level 4.**
   - It is 1.1–1.9 Hits below the reference at levels 0–3.
   - Sandstorm (2.4–2.6 on the Jinn) more than pays that back.
5. **Brutes:**
   - The Basilisk and Minotaur bodies are strong at levels 0–1 (+0.9 to +1.4) and average
     after.
   - The Gladiator's body is 0.5–1.2 below the reference at every level, which runs against
     the owner's "brutes strong early".
   - Forked Metal Rod is worth 2.0–2.9 on every brute holder, against 1.4 on the reference.
     It is the strongest non-area card on the brutes, and very rare.
6. **Collections saturate, as the plan expected.**
   - By level 10 the typical inventory is within 0.4 of the unconstrained one for every
     monster but the Jinn. The Jinn's gap is 2.6, because its fourth Sandstorm is an epic
     still arriving. Past about 500 fights a player holds the best cards for their monster.
   - At levels 1–5 the gap is 2–7.5 Hits, largest for the Jinn and Angel. The copy limit of 4
     lets an unconstrained hand stack Sandstorm or Blink, which are about 2–3 each.
   - A lower copy limit on epics (the owner's rarity rule) would mostly narrow that gap. Test
     it in Layer 4.
7. **At levels 0–3, where production players are, typical inventories are close.** They span
   9.8–11.6 Hits. So early imbalance there comes mainly from bodies (the Angel, Dragon, and
   Jinn low; the Basilisk and Minotaur high) and from single epics, not from collections.

## Next steps

- **Layer 4 (task 9) searches hands** from these collections and judges the band on whole
  fights.
- **Candidate changes for the next pass,** not made here:
  - raise the Angel's and Dragon's bodies, or trim the Angel's top cards;
  - fix Tsunami and the Unicorn's four weak cards;
  - an epic copy limit.
