# Millefleur

![The Millefleur Workshop](samples/workshop.jpg)

## Artist statement

Every Deck Monsters theme until now has been a screen: a dark terminal in one colour of light.
Millefleur is a page. It is what the game looks like if it was kept in a binder by someone who
paints in the margins, under a soft light, in the colours of the unicorn tapestries: madder,
weld and woad, watered down.

It is gentle on purpose. The paint does the work, so the parts you touch can be quiet: one
typeface, one way to mark what is selected, one kind of paper. Colour appears where something
matters (the tab you are on, a natural 20, a level gained) and stays out of the way everywhere
else. A halo of five colours sits around each monster, the one piece of real magic on the page.

Underneath it all the game is still the game. The fights are still written in a terminal's
monospace, and the tabs and buttons borrow quietly from System 7. It is a theme for anyone who
found the terminal look cold, and who wants this odd bestiary to feel like it lives somewhere
lovely.

## The brief, in short

- **For whom:** players who want the game soft, light and beautiful, and more feminine than the
  terminal themes. It must be a choice in its own right, not a light mode.
- **Feel:** watercolour and paper, transparency, gentle beauty, consistency. "Magic leaking
  through office supplies", minus the clutter.
- **Inspirations:** watercolour, Japanese wash painting and abstract colour-field painting for
  the backgrounds; the Unicorn Tapestries for the palette; 90s Trapper Keepers and holographic
  stickers for the rainbow (used only as a fade); System 6/7 for the shapes of tabs, title bars,
  close boxes and the default button.
- **Hard rules:** text contrast of at least 4.5:1, the fight transcript stays monospace,
  nothing animates on the page, and no other theme pays for this one in bytes.
- **Name:** Millefleur, the flower-strewn ground of the tapestries, and the garden the Unicorn
  raids for roses. A dark sibling, if ever made, would be Gloaming.
